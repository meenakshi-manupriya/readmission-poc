# Decision Log: Data Engineering Phase

Dataset version: v_86a646f1 (119 features; train 79,567 rows; test 19,773 rows)
Reproduce with: python -m src.pipeline

## D1. Target
Y = 1 if readmitted == "<30", else 0. Readmission after 30 days (">30") and no
readmission ("NO") are both negative. Positive rate: 11.39% after exclusions.
Why: the spec defines the problem as early readmission. Cost: a patient readmitted
on day 31 is labelled the same as one never readmitted.

## D2. Cohort exclusions
Removed 2,423 encounters discharged as expired (codes 11, 19, 20, 21) or to hospice
(13, 14), plus 3 encounters with Unknown/Invalid gender. Final cohort: 99,340.
Why: a patient who died cannot be readmitted, so labelling them "not readmitted"
teaches the model about mortality instead of readmission risk.
Limitation: deaths after discharge, within the 30-day window, are not visible in
this dataset, so those patients remain labelled Y=0.
TODO: confirm the disposition codes against IDs_mapping.csv from the UCI download.

## D3. Row granularity and splitting
Every encounter is its own row (71,518 patients, 76.5% with a single encounter).
Train/test split uses GroupShuffleSplit on patient_nbr (test_size 0.2, seed 42):
zero patients appear in both sets. Positive rate: train 11.40%, test 11.35%.
Why: dropping repeat encounters discards real signal, and a random row split
would leak patients across train and test.
Deviation from spec section 5: a temporal split is NOT used as the primary split.
encounter_id is only a rough time proxy, and a temporal cut would reintroduce
patient overlap. Temporal validation is deferred to a later robustness check.

## D4. Split before feature engineering
Anything learned from data (category lists, kept-drug list, payer cutoff) is
decided from the training set only, then frozen in code. Test conforms to train.
Why: otherwise test-set information leaks into the design.

## D5. Loading bug fix
pandas read_csv converted the string "None" (test not performed) to NaN in
max_glu_serum and A1Cresult. Restored to "None" because the dataset metadata states
these columns have no missing values.

## D6. Dropped columns
weight (96.8% missing); citoglipton, examide (constant). readmitted, encounter_id
and patient_nbr are never features (target and identifiers).

## D7. Feature engineering (119 columns)
- Demographics (9): age band converted to its numeric midpoint (assumes roughly
  even spacing); race and gender one-hot, race_nan kept as an explicit column.
- Encounter (33): true counts kept numeric; admission_type_id and
  admission_source_id one-hot (they are codes, not magnitudes).
- Diagnoses (30): each of diag_1/2/3 grouped from ~700 ICD-9 codes into 9
  clinical categories plus Missing. V and E codes fall into Other.
- Medications (36): 8 drugs with enough usage keep their own status columns; the
  13 rare drugs contribute only through n_drugs_prescribed and n_drugs_changed.
  The cutoff was a judgment call made from training-set rates.
- Payer (11): 9 codes with at least ~1% of rows, plus Other and Missing.

## D8. payer_code
Timing of when this field is finalized relative to discharge could not be verified
from the data. Included, and its effect will be measured by training with and
without it (drop columns starting with "payer_"). Abbreviations are deliberately
not expanded here until confirmed from an authoritative source.

## D9. Reproducibility
The dataset version ID is a content hash. The same ID was produced in the notebook,
a second notebook run, and a fresh terminal process. The loader re-verifies hashes.

## Known limitations
- Data covers 1999-2008 with ICD-9 coding; care patterns have changed.
- Readmission is only observable within the dataset's hospital network.
- ICD-9 category boundaries follow the commonly used grouping for this dataset;
  verify against the published source before reporting.
- Single-feature correlations with Y are weak (max 0.16, number_inpatient), so
  modest model performance is expected.

## D10. Model families (training-set grouped 5-fold CV; test set untouched)
Compared logistic, logistic + degree-2 terms, random forest, HistGB, XGBoost, MLP.
AUROC: XGBoost 0.6446, RF 0.6433, HistGB 0.6426, logistic_poly2 0.6410,
MLP 0.6358, logistic 0.6352. Paired against logistic, the trees were ahead in
5/5 folds by 0.007-0.009. XGBoost vs RF: tied. XGBoost vs HistGB: +0.0021 in
5/5 folds, likely a regularization difference.
Shortlist: logistic (interpretable reference), XGBoost, random forest.
Dropped: HistGB (same family as XGBoost), poly2 (subset of what trees capture),
MLP (no consistent gain). Different algorithms converge near 0.64, which suggests
the features, not the method, set the ceiling.
Not covered: SVM (does not scale; no native probabilities), kNN, Naive Bayes.

## D12. Hyperparameter tuning (training-set grouped CV only)
XGBoost: 20-config random search. Top 7 configs span AUROC 0.6450-0.6469, within
fold noise; depth 3 dominated. Hand-picked baseline 0.6446. Selection rule fixed in
advance: among near-ties choose the simplest. Chosen: depth 3, lr 0.027, 545 trees,
min_child_weight 15, subsample 0.806, colsample 0.646, reg_lambda 2.18.
Tuned CV scores are optimistically biased (winner's curse); the unbiased estimate
comes from the held-out test set, used once.
Logistic C and RF min_samples_leaf swept for fairness: [results].

## D13. Extra feature columns (discharge_disposition_id, medical_specialty, A1Cresult, max_glu_serum)
These were classed as known-at-discharge in the audit but omitted from the first
feature set. Paired CV vs the 108-column set (same folds), bar +0.003 and >=4/5 folds:
logistic +0.0194 (5/5), XGBoost +0.0245 (5/5).
Ablation, XGBoost: disposition +0.0237, specialty +0.0007, A1C +0.0005, glucose +0.0002.
Robustness: merging codes 2 and 5 into Other -> +0.0224; merging 18 and 25 -> +0.0229.
Mapping file: 18 has no destination label (likely a null), 25 = "Not Mapped".
Kept: disposition only (117 features). Dropped: specialty, A1C, glucose.
Limitation: codes 18 and 25 record missing destination information, not a destination.

## D14. Final model settings (117 features; training-set grouped CV, test untouched)
AUROC: XGBoost hand-picked 0.6705, XGBoost tuned 0.6697, RF leaf20 0.6696,
logistic C=0.01 0.6589.
Tuned vs hand-picked XGBoost: -0.0009, positive in 1/5 folds. The tuning edge seen
on the old 108 features did not survive the feature change (winner's curse), so the
hand-picked settings are frozen. RF vs tuned XGBoost: -0.0001, tied.
XGBoost vs logistic: +0.0108, 5/5 folds.
Frozen: logistic C=0.01; XGBoost 300 trees, lr 0.05, depth 4, subsample 0.8,
colsample 0.8; RF 300 trees, min_samples_leaf=20.

## D15. Feature completeness audit
Ledger check: every column in the cleaned data has an explicit status (27 used, 13
partly used, 4 excluded by design, 4 tested and dropped); no unaccounted columns.
The earlier leakage table mislabelled Y as known-at-discharge (a default branch);
no downstream effect, since the feature modules select columns by name.
Tested finer diag_1 detail (22 common 3-digit ICD-9 codes + Other + Missing, 24 cols;
codes chosen by frequency, not outcome). Paired vs 117 features, same folds, bar
+0.003 and >=4/5 folds: logistic +0.0013 (5/5), XGBoost +0.0015 (4/5). Both fail.
Dropped. Final feature set: 117 columns.
Not tested: the 13 rare drugs individually, diag_2/diag_3 detail, derived features
(e.g. total prior visits). Not expected to matter, given the above.
Re-search on the 117-column features, confirmed on new folds: no gain, hand-picked retained.

## D16. Calibration (out-of-fold training predictions; calibrators scored by a second grouped CV)
Slope (1 = perfect): logistic 1.03, XGBoost 1.01, random forest 1.37 (underconfident).
Mean predicted risk within 0.0005 of the observed 0.1140 for all three.
Rule set in advance: adopt a calibrator only if out-of-fold Brier falls by >= 0.0002.
XGBoost: neither helps. Random forest: sigmoid -0.0004 (slope 1.00, AUROC unchanged),
isotonic same Brier but AUROC -0.0011. Logistic: isotonic -0.0002 (at the bar) but
AUROC -0.0020; left uncalibrated.
Final: XGBoost uncalibrated, random forest + sigmoid, logistic uncalibrated.
Observed: logistic overestimates risk in its lowest decile (about 5.8% vs 3.9%).
Open: final calibration check on the test set.
